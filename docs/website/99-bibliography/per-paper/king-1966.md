# King (1966)

```{admonition} The structure of star clusters. III. Some simple dynamical models
:class: note

**Authors.** Ivan R. King (Berkeley Astronomy Department)

**Reference.** *The Astronomical Journal* **71**, 64–75 (1966); received 20 October 1965.

**ADS.** [1966AJ.....71...64K](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1966AJ.....71...64K) ·
**DOI.** [10.1086/109857](https://doi.org/10.1086/109857)
```

## Abstract (paraphrased)

Presents spatially-limited dynamical models of star clusters from steady-state solutions of
the Fokker–Planck equation, corresponding to the tidal cutoff imposed by the Galaxy. The
velocity distribution is a lowered (truncated) Maxwellian; solving Poisson's equation
self-consistently yields a family of single-mass, isotropic density profiles parameterised by
the dimensionless central potential $W_0$. The projected densities resemble those of open
clusters, globular clusters, and elliptical galaxies.

## Model in brief (verified against the paper)

- **Distribution function** (Eq. 3): at the centre $f(0,v) = k[\exp(-j^2 v^2) - \exp(-j^2 v_e^2)]$
  — a Maxwellian *lowered* by a constant so $f$ and the density vanish at the escape velocity
  $v_e^2 = -2V$ (Eq. 5). Energy $E = \tfrac12 v^2 + V(r)$ (Eq. 4).
- **Dimensionless potential** $W \equiv -2j^2 V$ (Eq. 9), with central value $W_0$; $W \to 0$ at
  the tidal boundary. The density follows $\Psi(W) = e^{W}\!\int_0^{W} e^{-\eta}\eta^{1/2}\,d\eta$
  (Eq. 17), proportional to $\rho$.
- **Poisson equation** in the dimensionless radius $R = r/r_c$ (Eq. 13):
  $\dfrac{d^2 W}{dR^2} + \dfrac{2}{R}\dfrac{dW}{dR} = -9\,\dfrac{\rho}{\rho_0}$ (Eq. 16). The
  **factor of 9** comes from fixing the central $\nabla^2 W = -9$, i.e. $8\pi G j^2 r_c^2 \rho_0 = 9$
  (Eq. 15); King notes the scale factor $r_c$ is then very close to the observational "core
  radius". Central boundary conditions: $dW/dR = 0$ and $\lim_{R\to 0}(2/R)\,dW/dR = -6$ (Eq. 18).
- **Concentration.** The model truncates at the radius $R = r_t/r_c$ where $W$ (and $\rho$) reach
  zero. The **concentration parameter is $c \equiv r_t/r_c$**, and the quantity usually quoted is
  $\log_{10} c = \log_{10}(r_t/r_c)$ (King's Table II tabulates both). Higher $W_0$ ⇒ higher
  concentration; as $W_0 \to \infty$ the model approaches the isothermal sphere.

## Notes

The right choice for old globular clusters where the tidal-radius constraint is observationally
well-defined. {cite:t}`Gieles2015` (LIMEPY) generalises the lowered-isothermal family to
multi-mass, anisotropic, and rotating populations.
