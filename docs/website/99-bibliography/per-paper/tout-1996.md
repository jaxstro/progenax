# Tout, Pols, Eggleton & Han (1996)

```{admonition} Zero-age main-sequence radii and luminosities as analytic functions of mass and metallicity

**DOI.** [10.1093/mnras/281.1.257](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/281.1.257) ·
```

## Abstract (paraphrased)

Presents strictly analytic fitting formulae for the zero-age main-sequence (ZAMS) radius and
luminosity of stars as functions of mass and metallicity. The fits are built from a grid of
600 ZAMS stellar models computed with the Eggleton evolution code and OPAL/Alexander
opacities, spanning $0.1 \le M/M_\odot \le 100$ and $0.0001 \le Z \le 0.03$. Both relations
take the form of rational functions in mass with strictly **positive** coefficients (so the
denominators never vanish), and each coefficient is itself a low-order polynomial in
$\log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot)$. The formulae are everywhere continuous and differentiable in both
mass and metallicity — important for luminosity-function and number-count work, which depends
on the *gradient* of the mass–luminosity relation.

## The formulae (Eqs. 1–4, verified against the PDF, p. 258)

**Luminosity (Eq. 1):**

```{math}
:label: tout-luminosity
\frac{L}{L_\odot} = \frac{\alpha M^{5.5} + \beta M^{11}}
{\gamma + M^{3} + \delta M^{5} + \epsilon M^{7} + \zeta M^{8} + \eta M^{9.5}},
```

**Radius (Eq. 2):**

```{math}
:label: tout-radius
\frac{R}{R_\odot} = \frac{\theta M^{2.5} + \iota M^{6.5} + \kappa M^{11} + \lambda M^{19} + \mu M^{19.5}}
{\nu + \xi M^{2} + o\,M^{8.5} + M^{18.5} + \pi M^{19.5}},
```

with $M \equiv M/M_\odot$. The non-integer powers ($M^{5.5}$, $M^{2.5}$, $M^{6.5}$, …) are
deliberate: the authors found that pure integer powers could not fit the relations to the
required accuracy, so a $\sqrt{M}$ factor was introduced in eight places. Each coefficient
(**except** $\nu$) is a degree-4 polynomial in metallicity (Eqs. 3–4):

```{math}
:label: tout-metallicity
c = a + b\,\zeta + c'\,\zeta^{2} + d\,\zeta^{3} + e\,\zeta^{4},
\qquad \zeta = \log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot),\quad Z_\odot = 0.02.
```

The coefficient $\nu$ is **fixed at its $Z = 0.02$ optimum for all metallicities** (PDF
p. 258): some radius-fit coefficients varied by orders of magnitude across $Z$ and threatened
to drive the rational function negative, so $\nu$ was held $Z$-independent to stabilise the
fit. At solar metallicity ($\zeta = 0$) every coefficient equals its column-$a$ value.

## Validity and accuracy (abstract + §3, verified)

- **Mass range:** $0.1 \le M/M_\odot \le 100$. (Extrapolation beyond gives "inaccurate but
  still reasonable" numbers.)
- **Metallicity range:** $0.0001 \le Z \le 0.03$. **Metallicity extrapolation must be
  avoided** — outside the fitted range the rational functions can go negative.
- **Accuracy:** luminosity errors generally $< 7.5\%$ ($< 3\%$ at solar $Z = 0.02$); radius
  errors $< 5\%$ ($< 1.2\%$ at solar).

## Solar ZAMS anchors (computed from the verified coefficients at $M = 1$, $Z = 0.02$)

- $L(1\,M_\odot) = (\alpha + \beta)/(\gamma + 1 + \delta + \epsilon + \zeta + \eta)
  \approx 0.70\,L_\odot$
- $R(1\,M_\odot) = (\theta + \iota + \kappa + \lambda + \mu)/(\nu + \xi + o + 1 + \pi)
  \approx 0.89\,R_\odot$

These are the **ZAMS** Sun (slightly under-luminous and more compact than today's
$L = R = 1$); progenax's ZAMS tests anchor on these values rather than present-day solar.

## Use in progenax

- `progenax.stellar` — ZAMS $L(M,Z)$ {eq}`tout-luminosity` and $R(M,Z)$ {eq}`tout-radius`;
  the verified literals live in `_TOUT_L_COEFFS` (Table 1), `_TOUT_R_COEFFS` and `_TOUT_R_NU`
  (Table 2).
- [](../../50-validation/zams-relations.md) — validation of the ZAMS relations against the
  paper's accuracy claims and the solar ZAMS anchors above.
- Used by the binary / dynamical-mass workflows that need stellar luminosities and radii from
  the sampled masses (e.g. mass–luminosity conversion for binary populations and
  dynamical-mass diagnostics).

## Notes

`progenax.stellar` clips the input metallicity to $[10^{-4},\,0.03]$ before evaluating the
fits — this is the paper's own explicit guidance (avoid $Z$ extrapolation), not an arbitrary
guard. These ZAMS formulae are a present-day placeholder for the stellar physics that the
planned **startrax** (Hurley+ SSE/BSE) and **stellax** (MESA-on-JAX) packages will eventually
supply.
