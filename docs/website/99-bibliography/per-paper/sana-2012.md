# Sana et al. (2012)

```{admonition} Binary interaction dominates the evolution of massive stars
:class: note

**Authors.** H. Sana, S. E. de Mink, A. de Koter, N. Langer, C. J. Evans, M. Gieles,
E. Gosset, R. G. Izzard, J.-B. Le Bouquin, F. R. N. Schneider.

**Reference.** *Science* **337**, 444–446 (2012).

**DOI.** [10.1126/science.1223344](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1223344) ·
**ADS.** [2012Sci...337..444S](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012Sci...337..444S)
```

## Abstract (paraphrased)

A homogeneous spectroscopic analysis of the O-star population of six nearby Galactic open
clusters (71 single + multiple O-type objects), sensitive to orbital periods up to ~10 yr.
Simultaneously measures the intrinsic binary fraction and the period, mass-ratio, and
eccentricity power-law distributions, correcting for observational biases via Monte-Carlo
simulation. Concludes that over 70% of all massive stars exchange mass with a companion
(one third merging), so binary interaction dominates massive-star evolution.

## Intrinsic distributions (verified against the paper, p. 444–445)

The observed binary fraction is $f_{\rm obs}=40/71=0.56$. After bias correction, the
**intrinsic** distributions (power laws in $\log_{10}P$, $q$, $e$ with exponents $\pi,\kappa,\eta$)
and binary fraction are

```{math}
:label: sana-params
f_{\rm bin} = 0.69 \pm 0.09,
\qquad \pi = -0.55 \pm 0.2 \ (\text{period}),
\qquad \kappa = -0.1 \pm 0.6 \ (\text{mass ratio}),
```

valid for binaries with periods up to ~9 yr. The negative $\pi$ is a **strong preference for
close pairs** (Öpik-like but steeper); $\kappa \approx -0.1$ is a nearly **uniform mass-ratio
distribution** (no preference for equal masses, contra a "thermal" or twin-peaked $q$).
Integrating these implies 71% of O-type stars interact with a companion.

## Use in progenax

## Notes

The intrinsic O-star binary fraction $f_{\rm bin}=0.69$ is the value progenax's
`massive_stars()` factory adopts (not 0.70). Combined with [](moe-distefano-2017.md), which
extends the statistics to lower masses and resolves the period–mass-ratio interrelation, this
is the empirical foundation for massive-binary population synthesis.
