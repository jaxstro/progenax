# King (1962)

```{admonition} The structure of star clusters. I. An empirical density law
:class: note

**Author.** Ivan King (University of Illinois Observatory).

**Reference.** *The Astronomical Journal* **67**, 471–485 (1962), No. 1303. Received 1962 June 18.

**DOI.** [10.1086/108756](https://doi.org/10.1086/108756) ·
**ADS.** [1962AJ.....67..471K](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1962AJ.....67..471K)

**Verified.** The tidal-radius formulae (Eqs. 3, 11, 12) and the empirical density law (Eq. 2)
below were checked against the published PDF.
```

## Abstract (paraphrased)

Re-examines the density distribution in globular clusters from new Mount Wilson / Palomar star
counts. Jeans' $r^{-4}$ outer law is shown to rest on unjustified assumptions and to be poorly
supported by the data; instead the surface density drops to **zero at a finite tidal radius**,
as expected from galactic tidal forces. King proposes a three-parameter empirical density law
that fits the counts from centre to edge in clusters of all concentrations, and argues that
the same law describes both globular clusters and Sculptor-type dwarf galaxies. This is the
**first** of the King-model papers; the self-consistent lowered-isothermal dynamical model
appears in King (1966) (Paper III).

## The empirical density law (Eq. 2, verified)

For the outer parts, King fits

```{math}
:label: king1962-density
f = f_1\left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{r_t}\right)^{2},
```

a surface density that reaches zero at the **tidal radius** $r_t$ (the intercept of
$\sqrt{f}$ vs $1/r$). This finite cutoff — absent from a pure power law — is the empirical
signature of galactic tidal truncation that the later King (1966) dynamical models reproduce
self-consistently.

## The tidal / limiting radius from galactic tides (§III, verified)

King estimates the tidal cutoff $r_t$ from galactic tidal forces. Following von Hoerner (1957),
the **instantaneous** limiting radius of a cluster on a straight-line (radial) orbit is

```{math}
:label: king1962-rt-instant
r_t = R\left(\frac{M}{2 M_g}\right)^{1/3},
```

where $R$ is the galactocentric distance and $M$, $M_g$ are the cluster and galaxy masses
(Eq. 3). King explicitly notes a **typographical error in von Hoerner's Eq. 33** — a 2 printed
in the numerator instead of the denominator.

For the more realistic case of a cluster on an **elliptical** orbit, the cluster is truncated
most severely at perigalacticon $R_p = a(1-e)$, and the limiting radius there is (Eq. 11)

```{math}
:label: king1962-rt-perigal
r_{\rm lim} = R_p\left[\frac{M}{M_g(3 + e)}\right]^{1/3}.
```

For the elongated orbits typical of globular clusters, King adopts the **compromise** formula
(Eq. 12)

```{math}
:label: king1962-rt-compromise
r_{\rm lim} = R_p\left(\frac{M}{3.5\,M_g}\right)^{1/3}.
```

The classical Hill/Roche **factor-3** form $r_J = R\,(M_{\rm cl}/3 M_{\rm gal})^{1/3}$ used in
progenax is the **circular-orbit ($e = 0$) limit of Eq. 11** for a point-mass host. Because of
the $1/3$ power, none of the masses or the orbital eccentricity needs to be known accurately —
King stresses that this weak dependence is "especially fortunate" given the crudeness of the
inverse-square galactic-force approximation.

## Notes

This is the *empirical* density-law paper. The self-consistent **lowered-isothermal King
model** — the distribution function progenax actually integrates for King profiles and velocity
DFs — is King (1966), Paper III; see [](king-1966.md). progenax cites King (1962) specifically
for the **tidal-radius estimate from galactic tides**, not for the dynamical model.
